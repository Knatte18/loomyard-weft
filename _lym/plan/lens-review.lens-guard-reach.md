Guard-reach lens review of the slack-machine-bridge plan: for each guard, refusal, branch and test row the cards add, can a real entry point reach it, and does the guarded site recur unguarded?

1. **BLOCKING — Card 10 (D7, D3 restart settle): a lost settle leads to a second post.** `sendOne` writes the timestamp to `Accepted` and then settles delivered. On a lost connection the entry stays, and creel still holds the message pending. Every poll calls `TakeMachineOutbound` afresh, so take hands the message out again and `sendOne` posts it again. The same happens after a restart: `settleAccepted` keeps entries on a lost connection, and the poll that follows takes and reposts them. `send` never checks `Accepted` before posting. This breaks D7's "a restart never posts the message again once the record is written". Evidence: `plan:internal/slackengine#bridgeSender.send`, `bridgeSender.sendOne` step 5 and `bridgeSender.settleAccepted` in card 10. Fix: `sendOne` skips any id held in `Accepted` and retries its settle instead of posting.

2. **BLOCKING — Card 5 (D2, D3): a reply across machines skips D2's orch and button checks.** `postMachine` refuses a non-orch sender (step 4) and an orch whose `SendReady` fails (step 6). Card 5's `Reply` journals `machine-outbound` for a message whose `SendingMachine` is set "after the same line check" only. `Reply` requires only that the replier holds the message. `Forward` (card 5) lets machine mail go to any local strand, so a non-orch strand can hold it and reply across machines. An orch whose button is off, or whose machine lacks a token or setup, can also reply. That breaks D2 ("accepted only from an orch strand that has a slack button"; post and `post --reply` carry it) and D3 ("refused at once, before journaling, when the sending orch's slack button is off"). Evidence: card 5 Intent, the `Reply` paragraph; card 3's `postMachine` steps 4 and 6; `internal/creelengine#Store.Reply`. No row covers it. Fix: `Reply`'s machine branch runs the orch-sender check and `SendReady`, with a row for each.

3. **BLOCKING — Cards 8 and 9 (D6): the pin branch of `approve <machine> <app id>` cannot run against real Slack.** Card 9 lists every bot by calling `BotUsersNamed` with an empty name, "which keeps every bot". Card 8 defines `BotUsersNamed` as keeping only bot users whose `name` equals the given name. No bot is named "", so the real `API` returns none, and every `approve <m> <app id>` takes the "none of that app id" refusal. Card 9 does not list `API.BotUsersNamed` under Edit. Its row `APPROVE Neve A0UPPER` passes only because `fakeSlack` is in-memory. Evidence: card 8 Intent, the `BotUsersNamed` bullet; card 9 Intent, the `approve <m> <app id>` bullet.

4. **MEDIUM — Card 10 (D4, D7): a send-side notice mark can stay set for good.** A send-side mark clears only when "a hold ending, by a post or a changed reason" happens. A held message that creel returns at its hour, which take then stops returning, ends the episode with neither. The `<kind>:<local orch> <peer>` mark then stays, and the next `peer-unapproved`, `bridge-name-taken` or `bridge-archived` episode for that pair posts no notice, because `markBridgeNotice` answers false. D4 requires the mark to be "cleared when the episode ends". No row reaches a second episode after a return. Evidence: card 10 Intent, the `hold` / `markBridgeNotice` lines.

5. **MEDIUM — Card 11 (D4, D8): read-side marks are never cleared.** Card 11 sets `<kind>:<channel id>` marks for the from-clash, wrong-app, unpinned-hold, retired-orch and reply-to notices, but names no point where any of them clears. After `approve`, then `revoke`, then a new held message, the unpin-hold mark is still set, so the new episode posts no notice. D8 asks for one notice per held episode, not one per channel lifetime. Evidence: card 11 Intent, handle steps 6, 8, 9 and 11 and "Read-side notice keys…"; no `clearBridgeNotice` call site is stated, though card 11 lists it under Uses.

6. **MEDIUM — Card 11 (D8): a `DeliverMachine` refusal other than a reply-to refusal has no stated outcome.** Only a reply-to refusal is "skipped with a notice". A refused receiver, a refused sender, or a line over the cap is also a daemon refusal, and `IsDaemonRefusal` is a plain bool that cannot tell these apart from a reply-to refusal. If such a refusal holds the cursor, the channel stops for good. If it is skipped, nothing words its notice. Evidence: card 11 handle step 13; card 4's `IsDaemonRefusal` returns bool; card 5 `DeliverMachine` steps 2 to 4.

7. **LOW — Card 11 (D10): a bridge thread hint never wakes the poll.** `takeHints` diverts only a hint naming a bridge-map channel, or `bridge-resync`. A thread hint `<channel>_<ts>` for a bridge channel falls through to `readsForHint`, which removes it as naming no open channel. Thread replies then wait for the poll. Evidence: card 11 Routing paragraph; `internal/slackengine#readsForHint`.

8. **LOW — Card 12 (D4): a retry of a refused bridge archive can be unreachable.** `retire` skips an orch whose own entry is no longer open and which `forwarded` no longer lists (`internal/slackengine#channelKeeper.retire` loop guard). If the orch's own channel archive and `dropForwarding` stood while a bridge-channel archive was refused, later retires skip that orch, and its bridge channels are never archived. Card 12 says a refused bridge archive "marks the orch refused, as for its own channel", but the retry never reaches it. Evidence: card 12 Intent; `internal/slackengine#channelKeeper.retire`.

9. **LOW — Card 4 (D3): `SettleMachineDelivered` for an id creel no longer holds has no stated answer.** A relay restarted after more than a week can call it for a message whose entry retention has pruned. If the call refuses, `settleAccepted` drops the entry, which is correct, but neither card states the answer and no row reaches it. Evidence: card 4 Intent, the `SettleMachineDelivered` bullet; card 10 `settleAccepted`.

10. **LOW — Card 11 (D8 resync): the source of the creator's app id is unstated.** For a channel whose creator bot has no pin, the notice must name "the creator's app id". `ChannelInfo.Creator` is a user id, but `BotAppID` takes a bot id and `BotUsersNamed` takes a name. Card 11 does not say how the app id is found. Evidence: card 11 Resync step 2; `internal/slackengine#API.BotAppID`; `internal/slackengine#ChannelInfo`.
